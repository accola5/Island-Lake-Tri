Getting there & packet pickup

Island Lake Lodge is located at 1995 Island Lake Lodge, a few minutes away from downtown Barnes, WI. Packet pickup is available Friday night. The Lodge itself is completely full, but campsites are available on a first-come, first-served basis. Parking is available all weekend at the Lodge.

Letter from the Race Director

Welcome to the inaugural Island Lake Triathlon!

Whether you are here to claim cabin bragging rights, chase a segment crown, or simply enjoy a morning of sweat and lake air, we could not be more thrilled to kick off this weekend together. The course is set, the water is waiting, and the competition is shaping up to be legendary.

The Contenders: An Absolutely Cracked Field

Our inaugural starting line features a little bit of everything: seasoned endurance royalty, marathon tune-ups, and wild cards with mysterious training regimens.

The Veteran Frontrunners: Josh Overturf leads the pack into the water, riding high on a recent PR at Ironman 70.3 Madison. Keep your eyes peeled for four Twin Cities Marathon hopefuls tuning their legs for next weekend.

The Dark Horses and Strategists: Max Tweedale enters following an injury-riddled training block and an alleged all-time low VO2 max. Despite a respectable 5th place at the Trail Loppet Half, rumors confirm Max is saving his legs to contest the coveted Accola Loop segment rather than the overall podium. Meanwhile, newcomers Chris and Stuart arrive looking to turn heads and make an immediate statement.

The Women's Field: Bridget Greiber, multi-70.3 Ironman extraordinaire, steps onto the dock as the heavy favorite. Ready to hunt her down are Dexcom Warriors Alison Accola and Emily Gruen, who plan to push the pace from gun to tape as they prepare for the upcoming winter cross-country ski season.

The Mystery Duo: The Coquyt couple represents the true wild card of the weekend. Nobody has spotted their workouts as they are "Strava Silent," though whispers claim they spent the entire summer hammering two-a-day sessions on the steep grades of Afton Alps.

The Kids Triathlon: Looking to steal the spotlight, Cal and Rosie are locked in an absolute barnburner of a head-to-head race. Expect fireworks from start to finish.

Course & Safety Notes

While bragging rights are permanent, safety remains priority number one:

Swim: Floatation aids are always welcome if needed. No one wins the race on the swim, so be careful.

Bike: Helmets are strictly mandatory before your bike leaves the rack. Ride defensively, yield to oncoming traffic, and take corners with respect — there is loose gravel.

Run: Stay hydrated, and give a loud cheer whenever you pass another competitor.

A Huge Thank You to Our Volunteers

A race like this cannot happen without our incredible volunteer crew. Thank you to Alison Accola for leading our volunteer crew and making sure everything was ready for the weekend. When you pass a volunteer on the course, be sure to offer a high five and a thank you!

Post-Race Festivities

Remember: across all divisions, the main objective of this weekend is having fun and making memories with great friends.

Immediately following the podium ceremony, the cabin kitchen and grill will be open and there will be a lasagna dinner celebration. Stick around for cold drinks, post-race food, lawn games, and race recap banter around the fire.

Race hard, look out for one another, and we will see you at the starting line!

Isaac Accola
Race Director, Island Lake Triathlon

Swim

Point-to-point across the water, starting at the Potawatomi Association Beach on the Island and finishing at the Island Lake Lodge Beach.

  • Start The swim begins at the Potawatomi Association Beach on the Island — plenty of parking there, so warm up with a walk or a short drive over.
  • Finish Point-to-point course, ending at the Island Lake Lodge Beach.
  • Required Swim caps are required for all racers.
  • Support Kayak support will be on the water the whole way across.
  • Option Not feeling the swim? A kayak, stand-up paddle board, or canoe is allowed instead — a time penalty will be assessed.
  • T1 After exiting the water, run up the hill to the driveway to reach the bike transition area.

Bike

Two clockwise loops out from the Lodge and back — watch the road markings, and know the map before you clip in.

  • Course Two clockwise loops, starting and finishing at the Lodge.
  • Markings Course markings will be on the road to help with navigation, but please review the map beforehand.
  • Required Bike helmets are required for the entire ride.
  • Off course If you find yourself on an unpaved road, you've gone the wrong way — turn back.
  • T1 exit Exit the driveway on the left (west) side onto the course.
  • T2 entry Enter the bike-to-run transition area on the right (east) side.

Run

One clockwise loop all the way around Island Lake. Short on paper — but don't let that fool you.

  • Course A single loop around Island Lake, run clockwise.
  • Heads up Don't be fooled by the short distance — the hills on this loop are serious.
  • Exit Exit the driveway on the left (west) side onto the course.
  • Finish Sprint to the finish line to qualify for the coveted Accola Loop Strava segment.
